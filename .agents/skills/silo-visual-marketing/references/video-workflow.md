# Video production, generation and editing

Use for shoot planning, new generation, existing-footage restoration, clip assembly, audio review and video delivery. Load [product context](product-and-service.md) and [physical fidelity](physical-fidelity.md) when Silo appears; use [brand voice](brand-voice.md) for spoken copy. Shared reference roles and protected-region decisions are in [visual workflow](visual-workflow.md).

## Plan the sequence and coverage

Distinguish a new generated scene, a repair of a recorded event and an edit assembled from existing takes. Establish required action beats, speaker/script, camera treatment, audio, aspect ratio and export scope. Preserve the requested medium; source stills can guide a video without changing the task into image editing.

For product demonstrations, make the supported states and contact sequence explicit. Distinguish contents needing a label from an existing-food reseal or basic-mode use. Use the documented pickup/labeling order and match required screen, light and sound beats to real evidence. An explanatory animation, fictional narrator or edited marketing clip cannot establish shipped device behavior.

For independently reusable topic clips, write entrances and bridges that work across the intended combinations. Prioritize essential demonstration and purchase-answer coverage so a shortened shoot still produces useful material. Group compatible takes, track done/retake status, and provide cue cards or teleprompter formatting when the production requires them.

Map footage/transcripts to exact clip IDs, takes, source angles and time ranges. Check camera-pair correspondence before selecting cuts; transcript content alone cannot establish a usable picture or matched angle.

## Condition generation inputs

Assign uploads to product identity/detail, style, action or literal first/end-frame roles. Verify the tool's supported modes before uploading. A product reference should teach identity without becoming the opening composition unless that is intended. If the tool requires a starting frame, prepare a faithful frame in the required composition first. Use reference labels in actual upload order when supported.

State which features each input supplies and which incidental features to ignore. Color/style conditioning must not silently dictate viewpoint, exposure, background or hand placement. Remove conflicting inputs or narrow their role when unwanted characteristics dominate; simplify around the required camera, exposure and treatment before reintroducing detail.

Use the source-anchored feature descriptions in [physical fidelity](physical-fidelity.md) in the actual video-generator instructions. Unexplained Silo part names do not establish geometry. Retain these constraints after prompt optimization, and describe which verified screen/ring/rocker states may change during the action while construction stays consistent. Review a prepared first frame before generating its dependent video; a flawed first frame can propagate the same channel, controls or lid defects through the clip.

Describe scene order, perspective, subject, action, surroundings and lighting with enough detail to preserve the intended scene. Keep necessary constraints when reducing prompt length. Use supported duration/aspect settings and explicit timing when the brief requires it; do not invent beat durations or assume a model follows them without inspecting the output.

Define which subjects move and which stay fixed. Preserve stationary food, hardware or background elements when the brief requires it. Maintain continuity across cuts and occlusion: product finish and form, lids and channels, food state, hand contact, wardrobe, set and props. Use supported action footage or keyframes for movement that text cannot establish.

## Preserve intentional effects

Treat a glow or particle visualization as a deliberate graphic layer with its own behavior. Specify how it survives the photographic lighting and color grade. Preserve emitted product light when removing glare. Verify that stylization has not changed real hardware, materials or screen/light states.

When animating particles already present in a supplied still, retain the scene's existing count and protected elements if the brief requests a closed scene. Define bounded displacement by region, direction and distance rather than a new continuous stream. Inspect for spawned particles, cascades, crossing between regions, food morphing and reversed movement. Require matching endpoint composition and inspect the seam when a seamless loop is requested.

Airflow must follow the documented container pathway: the airway belongs to the container wall and extraction uses the bottom connection. Do not substitute an air chamber in the base or evacuation through the rocker's release valve. Obtain matching diagrams for exact internal routing and small-part transitions; decorative particles do not validate a mechanism.

## Restore recorded footage

Define the permitted capture repairs and what stays locked through time. Stabilization, reflection/background cleanup and reconstruction preserve the recorded event, real deformation, hand contact, relative scale, geometry, display progression and emitted light. Do not replace the source event with a plausible generated action.

Track repairs through movement and occlusion using supported neighboring frames and source evidence. Reframing must not crop essential controls or hide the action. Preserve event duration and audio/action synchronization unless the brief permits a change. A clean poster frame or correct endpoints cannot establish successful restoration.

## Lock narration and check audio

Keep approved spoken text distinct from silent scene directions, device prompts and user replies. Preserve narrator perspective, intended voice and exact meaning. Attach literal spoken lines to their scenes when supported; make number pronunciation and units unambiguous. A first-person marketing character does not establish the device's actual dialogue.

Specify permitted voice, music, ambience and foley. For voice-only work, exclude other channels explicitly and ensure scene directions are not spoken. For a demonstration with a required release sound, retain the sound and its action synchronization.

Transcribe the selected take and compare the whole read with the locked script: body, numbers, claims, speakers and tagline. Listen for unwanted audio, missing speech, pronunciation, voice quality and synchronization. A correct tagline with a rewritten body is partial compliance; transcript accuracy alone does not establish clean audio or accepted visuals.

If native audio repeatedly rewrites a required read, generate the picture without it and add an approved recorded/TTS read when the available workflow allows. Draft quality or batches can support iteration when justified, but every selected take requires complete review.

## Assemble and review through time

Review spoken joins alongside visual continuity. References to food or an object must make sense in the assembled order; bridges, entrances and pronouns must remain clear across cuts. Listen to the assembled dialogue and check for collisions, repetition and broken context. Isolated scripts or take labels cannot verify the joined result.

Watch the full sequence when available, then inspect transitions, contact changes, occlusion, suspicious intervals and loop seams in detail. Confirm that the requested product/human action actually occurs; camera movement cannot substitute for it. Compare product geometry, transparency, controls, gauge, screen/ring progression, food and hands with the originals. Check intended pacing, camera movement and transition comfort, including abrupt motion and distracting drift. Correct stills must connect through plausible motion; sampled frames do not verify every interval or the audio.

During a focused experiment, judge the variable being tested without treating other defects as acceptance. Before delivery, check the complete picture, script, audio, mechanism and continuity. A usable voice track or approved camera direction is only one component.

Repair bounded defects when the source construction can be retained. If revisions accumulate contradictions or repeatedly damage correct regions, rebuild from the original scene and necessary constraints. Reinspect the whole sequence after each correction and state any motion/audio that remains unverified.

Apply the physical feature checklist across relevant states, occlusion/reappearance and edits after every pass. Correct channel, rocker, control, display and ring defects before presenting a final clip as faithful to the product, within the authorized production scope. If full temporal inspection is unavailable, identify the inspected coverage and keep unverified intervals explicit; selected stills or generator completion do not clear the video.

## Export and preserve project dependencies

Export only the intended sequence and time ranges, excluding raw-take tails or leftover timeline material. Measure actual dimensions, aspect ratio, duration, format and any size cap; open and play the final file. Check crop, captions and picture/audio synchronization at the delivered size.

Use the project's clip/version naming and retain candidates needed for review. Treat exports, editable projects, autosaves, media and subtitles as separate assets. Verify linked-media paths before moving, renaming or cleaning source files; media may live in Downloads or other working folders. Title/time proximity can shortlist a project but cannot prove that it produced a particular export.

Choose generator/editor capabilities from the current environment. Verify input modes, limits, quality/audio controls and run metadata rather than assuming that an older prompt, folder name or claimed successful recipe identifies the current model or settings.
