# Visual production and review

Use for image generation/editing, lineup restoration, outpainting and shared visual-review decisions. For footage, motion, audio, shoots and video editing, use [video workflow](video-workflow.md).

## Assign reference roles

| Role | Establishes | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Source canvas | Existing viewpoint, composition, product and lighting | Define allowed changes and protected regions. |
| Product/detail | Real geometry, material, controls, accessories and proportions | A different angle supplies details without replacing the canvas viewpoint. |
| Style | Lighting, palette and atmosphere | Incidental geometry, background, hands and camera do not become requirements. |
| Layout/inventory | Required items, count, placement, spacing and hierarchy | Generated rendering does not establish hardware construction or texture. |
| Action | Visible contact and movement from a real sequence | A still establishes a state, not the transition into or out of it. |

Match finish, container form and action state across product sources. If a generated canvas has structural defects, recover construction from real references while retaining the arrangement required by the brief. Text alone may be insufficient when the available generator cannot accept references.

## Protect the edit and inventory

Record the allowed region/change, protected features, viewpoint and canvas size. Carry these constraints into the actual prompt or editor operation. Include correct product parts, light ring, screen, glyphs, contents, object count and background where relevant.

Use supported masks or original-image layers to confine local edits. Outpainting expands beyond the source edges while retaining the interior. Typography-only changes preserve product visibility, background and source dimensions. Do not promise unchanged pixels unless the workflow preserves them; compare and restore protected regions from the original when supported.

For a developed product-family lineup, enumerate every required item and placement before restoration. Keep its inventory and composition while repairing surface rendering from real product references. Do not discard added items, invent replacements or copy defective controls from the layout. Use matching references for accessories and scale. Compositing or bounded inpainting may preserve the arrangement better than full-scene regeneration.

## Build useful instructions

Specify physical identity, permitted changes, relevant construction, camera/view and scene treatment. Describe supported human actions with a start state, contact point, movement and end state. Use [product context](product-and-service.md) for behavior and [physical fidelity](physical-fidelity.md) for visible checks.

Build the generator handoff from inspected source details. Include plain visual descriptions and source assignments for the relevant channel, rocker, buttons, screen and light ring as defined in physical fidelity. Explain the allowed change and what must retain its identity; the generator receives the submitted inputs, not this skill or the agent's private reasoning. Do not shorten those details into unexplained product names. If a tool translates, optimizes or rewrites the prompt, inspect the actual candidate before applying/submitting it and restore dropped constraints or invented anatomy.

Keep camera, mood, container count, food, lighting and surrounding props consistent with the campaign. Check silhouette readability at the intended crop and focus: similar surfaces or defocus can erase the device while leaving the container visible. Adjust separation within the brief rather than prescribing one background or angle for all work.

When text is prohibited, distinguish overlays from intrinsic product lettering. Preserve physical branding; satisfy a literal no-readable-lettering requirement through angle, framing or focus where feasible. Clarify a material ambiguity before altering the logo. Verify any included claim or interface content separately from styling.

For a single text-field destination, provide one complete paste-ready prompt and keep analysis outside it. Preserve necessary constraints when shortening instructions. Use the tool's actual reference support and exposed limits; do not invent settings or assume prompt wording overrides the input mode.

## Compare and iterate

Inspect both the requested change and protected regions after every pass, including food swaps, background edits, typography edits and outpainting. Apply the [physical feature check](physical-fidelity.md) at whole-product and detail scale before presenting a product-bearing result as finished. Compare with the original, using an overlay/difference when useful. Separate unintended drift from the requested edit and legitimate rendering differences.

Keep the defect, comparison source and intended correction clear. Repair isolated defects when correct regions can be preserved. Rebuild from originals when geometry, material or several relationships are wrong, or when repeated edits damage correct areas. Choose the next step from the actual defect and progress rather than a fixed retry count.

Reinspect the whole result after each correction. Evaluate requested-change compliance, physical fidelity and creative quality separately; fixing one feature does not clear the other protected features. Preserve candidates needed for comparison using the project's naming practice. A liked intermediate or successful component does not establish acceptance of the complete deliverable. Report unresolved mismatches or inspection limits explicitly and retain candidate status rather than claiming product fidelity without verification.

Measure final pixels, aspect ratio, format, crop and any size cap. Open the delivered file and verify usability at the intended destination. Retain the source links needed to continue work and state material unresolved defects.
